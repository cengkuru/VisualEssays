The reports do not record a matching climb. Honduras at 98.1 percent notes the public is "unaware of the SISOCS portals". Uganda finds data sitting on donor platforms "which may not be known to the public". Malawi calls the information "not easily understandable to the general public". They slide left, and fall

One by one the circles move to the left wall and sink. A report can be 98 percent disclosed and still leave the public unable to find it. Height was never the same as reach.

This is the gap, in one frame

High on the wall, the data is published. Pinned to the floor, it stays out of public hands. Disclosure rose toward 100. The reach that was meant to follow did not. That distance is the whole finding.

What the reports say the fix is

Put the data where people are, in a form they can use, backed by law

If the gap is reach, the reports converge on closing it. Uganda's recommendation is concrete: "strengthen the use of community Barazas for citizens to build trust in Government's work," and "mainstream disclosure into the Access to Information Law." Publication was step one. Reach is the step the record keeps asking for.

Method and limits

Each circle is one CoST independent assurance report whose text states a disclosure or transparency rate (32 of the 95 ingested reports state one explicitly; the rest are excluded from the sort, not zeroed). Rates are quoted as reported in each document and not re-measured; confirm against the source before external citation. Cross-country rates use differing indicator sets across years, so vertical position is directional, not strictly comparable. "Reach" is a collective finding stated across the reports (the public being unaware of portals, data on platforms they do not know, information not understandable), not a per-report number; the horizontal drop visualises that shared finding, it does not assign each report a reach score.
